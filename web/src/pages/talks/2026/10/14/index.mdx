<Slide id="roadmap">

## Roadmap

**Measure, localize, fix — in that order.** Ninety minutes.

1. **Baseline** _(~15 min)_ — what number are you optimizing, and what is the
   ceiling? Tokens/sec, MFU, and the roofline that says how much is left.
2. **Instrument** _(~25 min)_ — `torch.profiler` on a real run. What to
   capture, what to leave off, and how not to profile the warmup.
3. **Read the trace** _(~25 min)_ — separate compute from communication from
   data stalls. The three shapes you will actually see.
4. **Fix, in order** _(~25 min)_ — each optimization tied to the measurement
   that justifies it. Stop when the measurement says stop.

<SpeakerNotes>
The ordering is the content. Most teams arrive having already applied fixes
from step 4 without doing steps 1-3, which is why their speedups did not
materialize.
</SpeakerNotes>

</Slide>

<Slide id="mfu">

## MFU: how much of the machine are you using?

$$\text{MFU} = \frac{\text{model FLOPs per step}}{\text{peak FLOP/s per device} \times \text{step time}}$$

Both quantities are **per-device**, so `world_size` does not appear — in
data-parallel training every rank does the same work.

You do not have to derive the numerator by hand. `ezpz` counts it with
PyTorch's `FlopCounterMode` (actual ops, not a $6ND$ estimate) and looks up
the peak, including a dynamically computed one for Intel PVC:

```python
from ezpz.flops import estimate_model_flops, compute_mfu
flops = estimate_model_flops(model, input_shape)     # measured, not estimated
mfu = compute_mfu(flops, step_duration)              # peak auto-detected
```

Real anchor (ATPESC deck, AuroraGPT production chains): 20B ran **17-22%
MFU**, 2B ran **9-12%** — bigger model, better utilization, same machine.

## Instrumenting a real run

The four decisions that make a profile useful, and the flag for each:

| Decision                     | Why it matters                              | Flag                            |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Skip the warmup**          | step 1 is allocator + autotune, not your run | `--pytorch-profiler-wait/warmup`|
| **Capture a few steps**      | one step is noise; 100 is gigabytes          | `--pytorch-profiler-active`     |
| **One rank, not all of them**| 2048 ranks writing traces to Lustre          | `--rank-zero-only`              |
| **Pay only for what you read**| shapes/stack/memory each cost overhead      | `--record-shapes` etc.          |

## Before you reach for the profiler

A profiler changes the thing it measures and produces gigabytes you then have
to interpret. A per-phase timer answers "which quarter of the step" in five
minutes, and often that is the whole answer.

`ezpz` already ships these — verified present, never presented:

- `StopWatch` (`history.py`) — context manager, logs the delta, optional wandb
- `timeitlogit` (`distributed.py`) — decorator, logs per-rank under `timeit/`
- `History.update()` — the source of the `dt= dtf= dtb= sps= tps= mfu=`
  per-step lines in the older decks

## Hands-on

The good news: this needs **no new code**. The flags already exist.

```bash
# 0 · environment + install (same four lines as the ATPESC deck)
source <(curl -fsSL https://ezpz.cool/utils.sh) && ezpz_setup_env
uv pip install --no-cache --link-mode=copy "git+https://github.com/saforem2/ezpz"

# 1 · baseline: how fast is it now?
ezpz launch python3 -m ezpz.examples.fsdp_tp

# 2 · same run, instrumented
ezpz launch python3 -m ezpz.examples.fsdp_tp --profile --rank-zero-only
```

Step 2 writes `torch-profiler-rank0-step{N}-{timestamp}.json`. Open it in
Perfetto or `chrome://tracing` and you are looking at your own trace.

## What to take away

1. **Measure first.** A baseline and a ceiling, before any change.
2. **Localize.** The trace tells you which of three problems you have.
3. **Fix in the order the measurement justifies**, and re-measure after each.
4. **Stop when the measurement says stop** — not when you run out of ideas.
