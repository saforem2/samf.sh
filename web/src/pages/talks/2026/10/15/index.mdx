## More nodes is not a fix

Scaling an inefficient run scales the inefficiency.

GPU idle **40%** of the step → 512 more nodes buys you 40% idle on 512 more
nodes. Same fraction, bigger bill.

> [!IMPORTANT]+ First question, before any change
> Is the GPU busy?

<Slide id="what-to-measure">

## Pick the number first

| Metric | What it tells you | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **tokens/sec/GPU** | what you are paying | the headline |
| **step time** | decomposes into phases | debugging |
| **MFU** | how much machine is left | knowing when to stop |

The trap: **2× on a kernel that is 15% of the step is a 7% win.** Speed up
the thing that dominates, or do not bother.

{/*───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
6 · MFU, concretely
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────*/}

<Slide id="mfu">

## MFU: how much of the machine are you using?

$$\text{MFU} = \frac{\text{model FLOPs}}{\text{peak FLOP/s} \times \text{step time}}$$

Both per-device, so `world_size` drops out. Every rank does the same work.

Do not hand-derive the numerator. `ezpz` counts it with `FlopCounterMode`
(real ops, not $6ND$) and looks up the peak, computing Intel PVC dynamically:

```python
from ezpz.flops import estimate_model_flops, compute_mfu
flops = estimate_model_flops(model, input_shape)   # counted, not estimated
mfu = compute_mfu(flops, step_duration)            # peak auto-detected
```

AuroraGPT production chains: 20B at **17-22%**, 2B at **9-12%**. Bigger
model, better utilization, same machine.

> [!WARNING]+ Published MFU assumes dense transformers
> Your model probably is not one. Compare against your own baseline, not a
> paper.

<SpeakerNotes>
The 20B/2B inversion surprises people: the bigger model is "slower" in
tokens/sec/GPU and better in MFU, because bigger matmuls keep the XPUs busy.
Source: ATPESC deck, all-production-perf.svg.
</SpeakerNotes>

</Slide>

<Slide id="roofline">

## The ceiling: roofline

Arithmetic intensity (FLOP per byte) against achievable FLOP/s. Two regimes,
two different fixes:

- **Memory-bound** — left of the ridge. Fusion, precision, layout.
- **Compute-bound** — right of it. Bigger matmuls, better kernels.

Knowing which side you are on tells you which half of this talk applies.

<Slide id="torch-profiler">

## Instrument it

Four decisions, four flags:

| Decision | Why | Flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| skip the warmup | step 1 is allocator + autotune | `--pytorch-profiler-warmup` |
| capture a few steps | 1 is noise, 100 is gigabytes | `--pytorch-profiler-active` |
| one rank | 2048 ranks × traces → Lustre | `--rank-zero-only` |
| pay for what you read | shapes/stack/memory cost | `--record-shapes`, ... |

Defaults: `wait=1 warmup=2 active=3 repeat=5`.

> [!IMPORTANT]+ Profiling the warmup is the classic mistake
> Step 1 is allocator churn and autotuning. It is not your training step.

<Slide id="lightweight-timers">

## Before you reach for the profiler

A profiler perturbs what it measures and hands you gigabytes to interpret. A
timer around each phase answers *which quarter of the step* in five minutes.

Often that is the whole answer.

```python
from ezpz.history import StopWatch
with StopWatch("fwd", iter=step):
    loss = model(batch)
```

`ezpz` also has `timeitlogit` (decorator, logs per-rank) and `History.update()`,
which is where the `dt= dtf= dtb= sps= tps= mfu=` log lines come from.

<SpeakerNotes>
Demo exactly one of these. Three ways to time a function is worse than one
that works.
</SpeakerNotes>

</Slide>

## Reading a trace

`--profile` drops `torch-profiler-rank0-step{N}-{timestamp}.json`. Open it in
[Perfetto](https://ui.perfetto.dev) or `chrome://tracing`.

What you are looking at: CPU rows on top (what Python asked for), device rows
below (what actually ran). A **gap in the device row is the GPU waiting.**

Find one step boundary, then measure the gaps inside it.

<Slide id="three-shapes">

## Three shapes, three fixes

| Trace shows | Diagnosis | Fix section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| idle at the **start** of every step | data stall | dataloader, FS |
| idle **mid**-step, regular | communication | collective, overlap |
| **busy** but throughput low | kernel efficiency | precision, fusion, shapes |

That is the whole diagnostic. Everything after this slide is one of three
branches.

## Fix: data stalls

Usually the cheapest big win, and the one teams skip because it is not the
interesting part.

- **Workers, prefetch, pinned memory** — the boring three, in that order
- **4M small files** is a metadata problem, not a bandwidth problem
- **Lustre does not like you** — one `stat()` storm × 50k ranks

> [!NOTE]+ Same failure as `import torch` at scale
> The fix is not a faster filesystem. It is not hitting the filesystem from
> every rank. See [`ezpz yeet`](https://samf.sh/posts/2026/05/01/).

## Fix: communication

- **Overlap** — the framework probably already does it; confirm in the trace
  before tuning anything
- **Bucket size** — too small is latency-bound, too large delays overlap
- **TP across nodes** is usually the bug, not a tuning problem

Keep `TP` on-node. `DP` is nearly free; `TP` is chatty.

<Slide id="fix-kernels">

## Fix: kernel efficiency

- **Mixed precision** — verify you got it; `bf16` in the config is not proof
- **`torch.compile`** — helps often, and on AuroraGPT MoE it cost **-35%**
- **Fused attention / optimizer** — usually free, check availability per device
- **Shapes** — pad to the tensor-core multiple

> [!WARNING]+ `torch.compile` is not free
> Compile time is real, and it does not always pay back. Measure it like
> anything else.
